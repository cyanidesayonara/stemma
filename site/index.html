How it works

  1. Import an MP3, WAV or FLAC file, or paste a YouTube link.
  2. Separate it into 2, 4 or 6 stems. It all happens on your PC; the first time, stemma downloads the models it needs.
  3. Practice. Mute your part, loop the hard bars, and slow them down or change the key while you learn them. When you have it, record your take over the band at the original speed and key.

Questions

What do I need to run it?

A 64-bit PC with Windows 10 (version 1809 or later) or Windows 11. stemma itself is a download of about 235 MB. The separation models download once, the first time you use each: about 67 MB for 2 stems, 180 MB for 4 stems and 120 MB for 6 stems, plus 83 MB for beat detection.

How long does separating a song take?

Every model uses the graphics card when Windows supports it (DirectML). On a fast desktop graphics card, a five-minute song separates into 4 or 6 stems in about a minute; the 2-stem mode (vocals and the backing track) is quicker still. Without a supported graphics card, separation runs on the processor and takes about as long as the song or longer. Separation runs in the background, so you can keep practicing while it works, and each song is separated only once.

Which files can I use?

MP3, WAV and FLAC files, or a YouTube link. You can export your own mix, a single stem or a loop as WAV or MP3.

Where are my songs and stems kept?

On your PC. Edit > Preferences shows the folder and lets you choose another one. The portable zip starts in %LOCALAPPDATA%\stemma; the Microsoft Store version keeps its files inside its own app folder, which Windows manages. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Is it really free?

Yes. There are no ads, no account and no subscription, and the source code is open under the MIT licence.

Windows warned me about the zip. Is it safe?

The portable zip is not code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen may show a warning: choose More info, then Run anyway. The Microsoft Store version installs without the warning. Releases from v2.6.0 on include a SHA256SUMS.txt file for checking the download.

Your songs stay yours

Separation runs on your own machine. Nothing is uploaded, there is no account and no telemetry, and once the models are downloaded stemma works offline. Separation uses your GPU when Windows supports it, and falls back to the CPU when it does not.
